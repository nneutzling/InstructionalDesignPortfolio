Not every sockeye goes to sea. This unit follows the ocean-migrating form of sockeye. But some sockeye, called kokanee, are landlocked — the exact same species, just spending their entire life in a freshwater lake instead of migrating to the ocean. Where an ocean-going sockeye spends its adult years feeding and growing in the Pacific, a kokanee spends that same stage of life feeding and growing in a lake instead. Kokanee are common in lakes right across the Columbia River Basin, including the Okanagan, and they still turn red and spawn in streams each autumn, just at a much smaller size.
